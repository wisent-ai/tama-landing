The operator's language: block-foreign-language-reply

An agent answers the operator in his language, in plain human sentences. tama hook block-foreign-language-reply is a Stop gate that refuses an answer in English to an operator who writes Polish.

How the language is read

No word list and no model decide which language a text is in; the alphabet does. English is written in ASCII letters alone, and Polish, like every language spelled with letters beyond ASCII, cannot be written at any length without them.

  • The operator writes such a language when a word of his own messages in his session carries a letter beyond ASCII. He often types without them, but not in every message of a session.
  • The answer is in it when a word of its own prose does. Its prose leaves out fenced code, code spans, every token that holds anything but letters (a path, an identifier, a number) and every line the operator wrote himself, so his own sentence repeated back is not read as the agent's language.

An answer whose prose has words and none of them carries such a letter, to an operator whose words do, is refused:

block-foreign-language-reply: BLOCKED: this answer is not written in the operator's language: his words carry letters beyond ASCII and none of the <n> words the answer writes itself has one, so it answers in another language than his (English to a man who writes Polish). Write it again in the language of his messages, in plain human sentences; code, paths and names stay as they are.

A subagent's session, whose answers go to its supervisor and not to the operator, is not judged; neither is a session whose operator never used such a letter. A transcript that cannot be read refuses nothing and says so on stderr. No model, no network, no lock.

Selection

The gate is declared and registered but not selected: it runs once the operator adds it to the enforcement selection (enforcement control).
