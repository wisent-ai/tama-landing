What the operator already knows

block-known-to-operator is a Stop gate that refuses a final message telling the operator what he already knows: what this session already told him, or what he himself decided — a refusal from his own hooks, a limit he set, a rule he stated — handed back to him as a blocker, a limitation, a reason the work stopped, or as news.

Why it exists

A refusal from the machine's own gates (one-off repairs), a build ration the operator set, or a rule he stated is his own decision, and a fact the session already told him is known to him. An answer that hands either back to him as a blocker, a limitation or news gives him nothing: the operator's rule is that a model should not provide the user information that was already communicated or that he decidedly asked for. block-repeated-blocker reads only Oko's defect register; this gate reads what the session told him and what he decided.

How it decides

Shape cannot decide this — whether a sentence restates an earlier answer, or reports the operator's own decision back to him, is a reading of two texts against a third — so the decider is a model, asked through Brama by the product's one route for that, ports::model_judgement (oko-cli proxy-call). The session is the memory. The gate reads the live transcript the stop payload names, or the newest transcript of the payload's session id, and puts three texts in front of the model: every message the operator wrote this session, every earlier answer the agent gave him, and the answer about to be sent. The model answers PASS, or BLOCK: followed by each offending sentence quoted verbatim and one clause saying whether it was told before or is his own decision.

What is refused

Sentence of the answerVerdict
Tells him in substance what an earlier answer of this session already told him — the same fact, failure, refusal or explanation, reworded or repeated Refused: what this session already told him is not news.
Reports back to him something he decided, asked for or installed — a refusal by his own hooks, gates or policy, a limit, quota or ration he set, a rule he stated, a choice he made — as a blocker, a limitation, a reason the work stopped, or as news Refused: what he decided is his intention, not the session's obstacle, and never needs telling.

The refusal is one {"decision":"block"} document on stdout. It names the gate, quotes the sentences the model named, quotes his rule verbatim, and says: A gate of his that refused you is his decision, not your blocker; what this session already told him is not news. Delete those sentences and answer with the work.

What passes

The results of the work he asked for. A direct answer to a question he asked. A defect identifier named in one clause. A failure outside his own decisions that no earlier answer mentioned. A sentence that only names what he must do or decide next, without explaining his own policy to him. A turn under stop_hook_active.

A session with no operator words and no earlier answer has nothing to be known yet: the model is not asked and the turn passes at no cost. A judgement nobody could obtain is never a denial: a judge Oko has recorded as not answering (judge-health.json, lifted by oko-cli transcripts tasks judge --probe) is not asked at all, and an absent Oko CLI, a refused gateway, an unreadable transcript or an empty reply is written to stderr as block-known-to-operator: no judgement: … and the turn passes, the same rule every model-judged gate follows. The binding carries a thirty-second timeout, as the other model-judged stop gate does: the registry's default ten seconds ends before the model answers.

Where the refusal comes from

Source: rust/crates/tama-hook-tools/src/ports/block_known_to_operator/mod.rs (the judgement) and session.rs (the transcript reading); binary: src/bin/block/completion/block_known_to_operator.rs; the real-binary journey that drives the gate over real session transcripts with a real judgement from Brama — the incident's own answer refused, the same work reported without it passed, a silent session never asked, a machine without Oko refusing nothing — rust/crates/tama-hook-tools/tests/known_to_operator_guard.rs; the declaration: hook-declarations/answer-discipline/block-known-to-operator.json. Declared with DEVICE_HOOK_EDIT_APPROVED=1 tama hooks declare --file <declaration> and installed with DEVICE_HOOK_EDIT_APPROVED=1 tama hooks release; which hooks this machine runs is decided by tama enforcement only <hook-id>…, which has to be run outside an agent session.
